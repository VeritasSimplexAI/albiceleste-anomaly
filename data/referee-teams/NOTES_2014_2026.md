# Officiating-crew data: FIFA Men's World Cups 2014, 2018, 2022, 2026

Compiled 2026-07-16. Covers the 2014, 2018 and 2022 tournaments in full, and the 2026
tournament through the semi-finals (102 of 104 matches played as of this date; the
third-place match on 2026-07-18 and the final, Spain v Argentina, on 2026-07-19 had
not been played and no crews had been announced on the sources used).

A companion file set for 1990-2010 is produced separately (files without the
`_2014_2026` suffix belong to that effort).

## Files

- `tournament_officials_2014_2026.csv` - 506 rows. Every official appointed to each
  tournament (including a small number who were appointed and later withdrawn or
  replaced - see "Withdrawals" below).
- `match_officials_2014_2026.csv` - 294 rows (64 + 64 + 64 + 102). Per-match crews.

Both CSVs are UTF-8 with BOM (open cleanly in Excel). Diacritics are preserved
(e.g. Szymon Marciniak, Cüneyt Çakır, Néstor Pitana).

## Method and sources

1. **Wikipedia officials articles** (raw wikitext parsed by script, not hand-copied):
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_officials
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_officials
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_officials
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_officials
2. **Wikipedia per-group and knockout-stage articles** for every tournament
   (e.g. "2014 FIFA World Cup Group A" ... "2026 FIFA World Cup knockout stage",
   "2026 FIFA World Cup round of 32"). Each match section carries a full officials
   block (assistants, fourth official, fifth/reserve official, and from 2018 the video
   crew), which Wikipedia sources from FIFA match report PDFs. Four matches live in
   dedicated articles and were parsed from there: Brazil v Germany (2014 SF),
   the 2014/2018/2022 finals, and "Battle of Lusail" (2022 QF Netherlands v Argentina).
3. **FIFA official lists (PDF)**:
   - 2018 original list of match officials, 29 March 2018 (via web.archive.org):
     confirms the original 36 referees / 63 assistants and resolves the Al-Jassim
     question (below).
   - "Final List of Match Officials FWC 2026" (v3, FIFA, mirrored at
     https://cdn.conmebol.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Final-List-of-Match-Officials-FWC-2026-v3.pdf):
     confirms the 52/88/30 rosters and supplied two VMOs missing from the Wikipedia
     table (Guillermo Pacheco MEX, Mohammed Obaid Khadim UAE).
4. **"Law 5 - The Referee" appointments blog** (law5-theref.blogspot.com), which
   republishes FIFA's per-matchday appointment sheets. Used only to fill the video
   crews (AVAR1/AVAR2-offside/AVAR3-support) for the 11 matches from the 2022 round
   of 16 onward where Wikipedia lists only the lead VAR. The lead VAR from this
   source matched Wikipedia in all 11 cases (cross-check passed). Also used to
   corroborate the 2026 QF Argentina v Switzerland crew.
5. Verification searches for individual officials (ESPN, the-AFC.com, gulfnews.com,
   football-italia.net, cultofcalcio.com).

No data was fabricated. Every cell traces to the source_url on its row; where a
second source supplemented a row, both URLs appear separated by "; ".

## Column semantics / caveats

- `match_date` is the **local stadium date** as shown in the Wikipedia match box.
  For 2026 (UTC-4 to UTC-7 venues) this can be one day earlier than the UTC date.
- `home_team`/`away_team` are FIFA's team1/team2 designations, not literal home advantage.
- `avar1/avar2/avar3` (2018 and 2022): FIFA's AVAR1 (assistant VAR), AVAR2 (offside
  VAR) and AVAR3 (support VAR), in that order where the source distinguishes them.
- **2026**: FIFA restructured the video team to three VMOs per match: VAR, one AVAR,
  and one "support VAR". In the CSV these are `var`, `avar1`, and `avar2`
  respectively; `avar3` is intentionally blank for all 2026 rows.
- Roles NOT included in the match CSV schema but present in the underlying sources
  (captured for Argentina matches in the appendix below): fifth official (2014),
  reserve assistant referee (2018-2026), stand-by AVAR (2022), stand-by VAR
  (2022 final; 2026 knockouts per law5).
- `role_pool` in the tournament CSV: `referee`, `assistant`, `video_official`
  (VAR pool, 2018+), and for 2014 only: `fourth` = FIFA's "support referee" (the
  fourth-official pool) and `support` = FIFA's "support assistant referee" (fifth
  officials). 2018+ fourth officials were drawn from the referee/assistant pools,
  so no separate fourth pool exists for those years.
- Confederation is derived from the official's country (so Neuza Back (Brazil),
  who worked in Stephanie Frappart's trio, is CONMEBOL, and Alireza Faghani is
  AFC via Iran in 2014-2022 and AFC via Australia in 2026).

## Structure evolution 2014 -> 2026

- **2014 (Brazil)**: referee + 2 assistant referees + fourth official + fifth
  official. FIFA appointed 25 referee trios plus 8 "support duos" (support referee =
  fourth official; support assistant = fifth official). Goal-line technology
  introduced; no VAR.
- **2018 (Russia)**: VAR introduced. Per match: referee, AR1, AR2, fourth official,
  reserve assistant referee, plus a video team of VAR + AVAR1 + AVAR2 (offside) +
  AVAR3 (support), operating from a central video operation room in Moscow.
  13 dedicated video officials appointed alongside 36 referees and 63 assistants.
- **2022 (Qatar)**: same 10-official structure, with 24 dedicated video match
  officials, semi-automated offside technology feeding the AVAR2/offside role, and a
  new **stand-by AVAR** appointed per match (backup in case the link between stadium
  and the centralised VAR room in Doha failed). First female officials at a men's
  World Cup: referees Frappart, Mukansanga, Yamashita; assistants Back, Diaz Medina,
  Nesbitt. (Frappart refereed Costa Rica v Germany.)
- **2026 (Canada/Mexico/USA)**: expanded 104-match tournament; 52 referees, 88
  assistants, 30 video match officials. Per-match video team slimmed to VAR + AVAR +
  support VAR (with stand-by VAR/AVAR appointments documented on appointment sheets).
  Six female officials (Penso and Katia Garcia as referees). On 2026-06-18 Penso,
  Mayo and Nesbitt formed the second all-female on-field trio at a men's World Cup
  (Czech Republic v South Africa). FIFA also made an in-tournament VAR procedure
  change before the semi-finals after controversial calls (ESPN, July 2026).

## Withdrawals / replacements (all included in tournament CSV)

- **2014**: Ravinesh Kumar (Fiji, assistant) injured pre-tournament, replaced by
  Mark Rule (New Zealand). Daniel Bennett (South Africa, support referee) missed
  the tournament through injury (his slot shows as vacant in the support duos;
  partner Aden Marwa (Kenya) remained).
- **2018**: Referee Fahad Al-Mirdasi (Saudi Arabia) withdrawn (attempted match-fixing,
  2018 King Cup final) together with assistants Mohammed Al-Abakry and Abdullah
  Al-Shalwai; no replacement referee was named, but assistants Hasan Al Mahri (UAE)
  and Hiroshi Yamauchi (Japan) were called up. Assistant Marwa Range (Kenya - the
  same official listed as "Aden Marwa"/"Aden Range" in 2014 sources) withdrew after
  a BBC bribery investigation; not replaced. Abdulrahman Al-Jassim (Qatar) was
  originally appointed **as a VAR only** (he is not among the 36 referees on FIFA's
  29 March 2018 list, which is headed by Al-Mirdasi) but took on-field appointments
  after the withdrawal - he appears in both the referee and video_official pools in
  the CSV.
- **2022**: no withdrawals found. (Mateu Lahoz and some others were released from
  duty after the quarter-finals, but that is rotation, not withdrawal.)
- **2026**: Referee Omar Artan (Somalia) is on FIFA's final list but was removed
  after being denied US entry in June 2026; he never officiated (FIFA paid his
  tournament salary). VMO Rob Dieperink (Netherlands) was dropped in May 2026
  following his arrest in England and replaced by Willy Delajod (France).

## Known gaps / quirks

1. **2026 final and third-place match**: unplayed at the 2026-07-16 cut-off; no rows.
   The final is Spain v Argentina (2026-07-19, MetLife Stadium); referee not yet
   announced on the sources consulted on the cut-off date.
2. **2022 knockout AVAR crews** (11 matches) come from the law5-theref appointments
   blog (which republishes FIFA appointment sheets) rather than Wikipedia; treat as
   very reliable but secondary. All lead VARs agreed with Wikipedia.
3. Stand-by AVAR (2022) and stand-by VAR/AVAR (2026) are not in the CSV schema;
   they are captured for Argentina matches in the appendix below.
4. 2014 fifth officials are not in the CSV schema (schema stops at fourth official);
   they are in the appendix for Argentina matches. The full 2014 per-match fifth
   official data exists in the parsed source if ever needed.
5. Name spellings follow the source of each row, so the same person can vary
   slightly across rows: e.g. Marwa Range = "Aden Marwa" (2014 officials list) =
   "Aden Range" (2014 match page); "Abdelhak/Abdelhalk Etchiali"; "Micheal Barwegen"
   (sic, per FIFA list); "Nicolas Taran/Nicolas Tarán". Fuzzy-match on surname when
   joining across files.
6. Guillermo Pacheco (MEX) and Mohammed Obaid Khadim (UAE) are absent from the
   Wikipedia 2026 VMO table but are on FIFA's final list PDF and appear in per-match
   crews; both are included in the tournament CSV with the FIFA PDF as source.
7. The 2014 officials article lists Ravshan Irmatov's 2014 assistants as Rasulov +
   Kochkarov (Kyrgyzstan); his 2018 trio was Rasulov + Saidov.
8. 2026 stage labels normalised: "Quarterfinals"/"Semifinals" (US spelling on 2026
   pages) -> "Quarter-finals"/"Semi-finals"; "Match for third place" -> "Third place
   match" across all years.
