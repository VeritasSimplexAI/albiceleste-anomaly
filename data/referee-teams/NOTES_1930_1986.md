# Officiating-crew data, FIFA Men's World Cups 1930–1986

Compiled 2026-07-17. Companion files (this batch covers the 13 tournaments 1930, 1934, 1938,
1950, 1954, 1958, 1962, 1966, 1970, 1974, 1978, 1982, 1986; the 1990–2010 and 2014–2026
batches are separate):

- `match_officials_1930_1986.csv` — 412 rows: one row per match
  (18+17+18+22+26+35+32+32+32+38+38+52+52), with referee and both linesmen.
  The schema stops at the two linesmen: **no fourth official existed in this era**
  (IFAB created the role in 1991) and there was no VAR.
- `tournament_officials_1930_1986.csv` — 395 rows: every official documented per tournament
  (the referee panel, plus linesman-only officials, plus host-federation/club linesmen known
  only from match records — distinguished in `role_pool`).

Both CSVs are UTF-8 with BOM. `source_url` lists every source that contributed to the row,
separated by `; `. Team spellings follow the fjelstul dataset exactly (West Germany,
Soviet Union, Dutch East Indies, etc.) because the match list itself is built from it.

## Sources

1. **fjelstul World Cup database** (local copy in `data/worldcup-1930-2022/`;
   https://github.com/jfjelstul/worldcup) — match list, dates, stages, team names, and the
   referee of every match (`referee_appearances.csv`), plus the per-tournament referee
   register (`referee_appointments.csv`) used for full names and confederations.
2. **RSSSF full-detail files** — `https://www.rsssf.org/tables/30full.html` … `86full.html`.
   The only source that records the two linesmen match-by-match for this whole era
   (format: `(attendance) Referee CTY, Linesman CTY, Linesman CTY`). Also used as the
   independent cross-check on every referee.
3. **Wikipedia tournament articles**, "Match officials" sections (1930 and 1950–1986;
   the 1934 and 1938 articles have no officials section) — appointed-panel rosters with
   full names, used to verify the fjelstul register and to identify linesman-only officials.
4. **linguasport.com** match pages (downloaded by an earlier session) — third-source
   spot-verification of referees and of the 13 disputed match dates.
5. Individual articles for targeted identifications:
   `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihaly_Ivancsics` (1934 final linesman),
   `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Escart%C3%ADn` (confirms exactly four 1934 linesman
   appearances, matching this data), `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup_final`
   (final crew), `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgardo_Codesal`,
   `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Taylor_(referee)` (negative check — see below).

Not usable: worldfootball.net / transfermarkt (anti-bot blocks, per the 1990–2010 batch);
FIFA.com historical match pages are offline.

## How the officiating teams were structured in this era

- There were **no dedicated linesman panels** at any World Cup 1930–1986. FIFA appointed a
  single list of referees; the two linesmen for each match were drawn from that same list,
  supplemented — heavily at some tournaments — by **host-federation (club) referees** who
  never appear on any published FIFA list. That is why full-name coverage tracks the share
  of panel members running lines: host-linesman-heavy tournaments (1934 Italy, 1938 France,
  1954 Switzerland, 1958 Sweden, 1962 Chile, 1966 England, 1978 Argentina, 1982 Spain) have
  the most surname-only entries. The first World Cup with a separate appointed linesman list
  was 1994 (see `NOTES_1990_2010.md`).
- **1930**: 15 officials in total (Wikipedia); 4 served only as linesmen (Rădulescu, Vallejo,
  Alonso, Aphesteguy — italicised on Wikipedia's list and blank in fjelstul's register).
  Neutrality rules were loose: Ulises Saucedo refereed and ran lines **while being Bolivia's
  head coach**, and Constantin Rădulescu ran lines while coaching Romania (both stated in the
  Wikipedia 1930 "Match officials" section). Langenus refereed 4 of the 18 matches.
- **1934/1938**: knockout format, with replays (flagged in `stage` as "… replay"). After the
  Anschluss, Austria's Alois Beranek officiated the 1938 tournament under the German
  federation — RSSSF codes him `GER (*)`, fjelstul keeps him under Austria; this file follows
  fjelstul ("Austria").
- **1950**: no knockout final — the deciding group is labelled `Final round` in `stage`
  (Uruguay v Brazil 16 Jul 1950 is a Final-round match, not a "Final").
- **1954/1958 group play-offs** are labelled e.g. `Group 2 play-off` (5 matches: GER–TUR and
  SUI–ITA 1954; NIR–CZE, WAL–HUN, URS–ENG 1958).
- **1974/1978/1982 second group stages** are labelled `Second round Group A` … `D`
  (fjelstul numbers the 1982 second-round groups 1–4; they were re-lettered A–D after
  verifying the group memberships against the official composition).
- Country/confederation notes: Ali Kandil appears as **United Arab Republic** (fjelstul's
  era-correct name for Egypt in 1966/1970); Arturo Yamasaki appears for **Peru** in 1962/1966
  and for **Mexico** in 1970 (as on Wikipedia's 1970 roster); Israel's officials are AFC in
  this era. Confederations for pre-1954 tournaments are anachronistic conveniences (UEFA,
  CAF, AFC, CONCACAF did not exist yet) — they follow the fjelstul register for consistency.

## Corrections and conflicts found (all documented, none silent)

**fjelstul data error corrected**: fjelstul assigns referee `R-245` **Nikolai Levnikov**
(a 1990s Russian referee, at USA 94/France 98 era) to six 1958/1962 matches — including the
**1962 final** — that belong to **Nikolay Latyshev** (Soviet Union). RSSSF records
"Latichev/Latischev ZSR" for all six, and Wikipedia's 1958 and 1962 "Match officials"
rosters list Latyshev (and no Levnikov). This file uses Nikolay Latyshev for: Sweden–Mexico
and the Wales–Hungary play-off 1958; England–Argentina, Italy–Switzerland,
Czechoslovakia–Hungary, and the Brazil–Czechoslovakia **final** 1962.

**13 RSSSF date typos** (fjelstul's date kept; each verified against linguasport and/or
Wikipedia): Cuba–Romania replay 1938 (rs 09.06.**34**), Yugoslavia–Mexico 1950 (rs 29.06),
Brazil–Sweden 1950 (rs 03.07), Wales–Hungary play-off 1958 (rs 15.06), France–West Germany
1958 3rd place (rs 26.06), Hungary–England 1962 (rs 31.**06**), Chile–Yugoslavia and
Brazil–Czechoslovakia 1962 (rs year "82"), Portugal–USSR 1966 (rs year "70"), East
Germany–Australia 1974 (rs year "94"), Netherlands–Argentina 1974 (rs 22.06),
Honduras–Yugoslavia 1982 (rs year "98"), Italy–West Germany 1982 final (rs 10.07).

**RSSSF country-code errors fixed** (person verified in a roster source): Kandil "ENG"→UAR
(1966), Yamasaki "POR"→Peru (1966), Álvarez "PAR"→Bolivia and Heyn "MEX"→Paraguay (1950),
Macko "GER"→Czechoslovakia (1958), Suppiah "SEN"→Singapore (1974), Vincenti "ITA"→France
(1954 play-off referee), Llobregat "VEM"→Venezuela (1974). Flagged with less certainty:
1934's "Baert FRA" (twice, as linesman) is taken to be the Belgian panel referee Louis Baert
— the same file lists "Baert BEL" as a referee and no French Baert is known.

**RSSSF spelling variants unified** (same person, same tournament): Latichev/Latischev
(=Latyshev), Ben Naceur (=Bin Nasser), Brossi (=Brozzi), Bronkhorm (=Bronkhorst), Rendil
(=Kandil), Danilo/Datillo (=Dattilo), Heyen (=Heyn), Biwerst (=Biwersi), Escheweiler
(=Eschweiler), Tschechner (=Tschenscher), Lienmayr (=Linemayr), Baretto/Barretto/Baretta
(=Barreto), Vincentini (=Vincenti), Riwersi (=Biwersi), Warnken/Wernken, and among
surname-only entries: Sdez→Sedez, Dragvoli/Dragvolt→Dragvoll, Büchmuller→Buchmüller,
Schicken→Schicker, Mogan→Morgan, Tosfaye→Tesfaye, Choi Duk-ryung→Choi Duk-ryong.
Kept distinct (possibly-but-not-provably the same): Andrén/Angrén (SWE 1958),
Marmo/Marino (URU 1962), Samidi/Seoudi (TUN 1978).

## Identification policy for linesmen (honesty about certainty)

1. **Tournament-scoped identification** (fjelstul register or that year's Wikipedia roster,
   same country): full name given — 644 slots.
2. **Targeted identification** with a dedicated source: Mihály Iváncsics (4 slots, 1934,
   incl. the final), Pedro Escartín (4 slots, 1934), Luis Paulino Siles (1 slot, 1982 — the
   only Costa Rican official at that tournament) — cited in the row's `source_url`.
3. **Register match across tournaments** (same surname + country in the fjelstul all-time
   register, person demonstrably active within 8 years): full name given but *inferred* —
   28 slots: Scarpi 1934; van Moorsel 1938; Lööw 1958; E. Marino, Rumenchev 1962; Biwersi,
   Eschweiler 1974; Ithurralde, Pestarino, Winsemann 1978; Soriano Aladrén 1982;
   E. Codesal 1986.
4. **Surname-only** (RSSSF's spelling, country from its trigram): 141 slots. Notable
   surname-only groups: 10 Italian club officials 1934; 11 French 1938; 11 Swiss 1954;
   9 Swedish/Norwegian/Danish 1958; 9 Chilean/other 1962; extra English Football League
   linesmen 1966 (Clements, Crawford, Howley, Taylor — "Taylor" may be Jack Taylor, the
   1974 final referee, but his Wikipedia article does not confirm a 1966 role, so no
   identification is made); Argentine host linesmen 1978 (Comesaña 5 slots, plus the
   panel referees Ithurralde/Pestarino identified above); "Sánchez (Spain)" 1982
   (3 slots — could be Sánchez Arminio or Sánchez Ibáñez, both era-plausible, left
   unidentified); "Garcia (Spain)" 1982; "Urres (Mexico)" 1986.

`tournament_officials_1930_1986.csv` `role_pool` values: `referee`,
`referee (also served as linesman)`, `linesman only`,
`linesman only (surname as recorded by RSSSF; not in FIFA panel lists)`, and
`appointed; no match recorded` (2 rows: José Luis García Carrión, Spain 1950; Werner
Winsemann, Canada 1970 — both on Wikipedia's rosters with no match record in any source).

## Notable, for the Argentina angle (all verified in this dataset)

Argentina played 41 matches in this era (absent 1938–1954 by withdrawal, failed to qualify 1970).

- **1930**: John Langenus (Belgium) refereed 3 of Argentina's 5 matches — Argentina–Chile,
  the semi-final v USA, and the **final v Uruguay** (linesmen Henri Christophe, Belgium, and
  Ulises Saucedo, Bolivia — the Bolivia head coach). Saucedo also refereed
  Argentina–Mexico (6–3), awarding three penalties.
- **1962**: the corrected **Nikolay Latyshev** refereed England–Argentina (group stage).
- **1966**: **Rudolf Kreitlein** (West Germany) refereed the England–Argentina quarter-final
  — the Antonio Rattín sending-off match — with Gottfried Dienst (Switzerland, referee of
  the 1966 final) and István Zsolt (Hungary) on the lines.
- **1974**: Jack Taylor (England) ran a line in Argentina–Brazil, then refereed
  Argentina–East Germany — nine days before taking the **final**.
- **1978**: Abraham Klein (Israel) refereed Argentina–Italy. **Sergio Gonella** (Italy) ran a
  line in Argentina–Peru (the controversial 6–0) four days **before refereeing the final**
  (Argentina–Netherlands, with Ramón Barreto, Uruguay, and Erich Linemayr, Austria).
- **1982**: Mario Rubio Vázquez (Mexico) refereed Argentina–Brazil (Maradona's red card);
  Nicolae Rainea (Romania) ran a line in Argentina–Hungary and then refereed
  Italy–Argentina in the second round.
- **1986**: **Ali Bin Nasser** (Tunisia) refereed the Argentina–England quarter-final ("Hand
  of God"/"Goal of the Century"), with **Bogdan Dotchev** (Bulgaria) and Berny Ulloa Morera
  (Costa Rica) on the lines. **Romualdo Arppi Filho** (Brazil) ran a line in
  Argentina–Bulgaria before refereeing the **final** (Argentina–West Germany, linesmen Erik
  Fredriksson, Sweden, and Berny Ulloa Morera — Ulloa having also refereed
  Argentina–Bulgaria and run the line in the quarter-final). Antonio Márquez Ramírez
  (Mexico) ran a line in Italy–Argentina and refereed the semi-final v Belgium.
- Argentine officials in the data: Macías (1930 referee, on Argentina's own squad list as
  attached referee per RSSSF); Brozzi (1958 referee); "Ventre" (1962 linesman, surname
  only); Goicoechea (1966 referee + linesman); **Coerezza** (1970: refereed Mexico–Belgium
  and the West Germany–England quarter-final, and ran a line in the Brazil–Italy **final**);
  Pestarino (1974 referee, 1978 linesman); Ithurralde (1978 linesman, before his 1982
  refereeing appearances); Comesaña (1978 host linesman, 5 matches); Espósito (1986:
  refereed Belgium–Mexico and Italy–France, ran lines twice).
- **Codesal dynasty**: José María Codesal (Uruguay) refereed at 1958 (Hungary–Wales) and
  1966 (Portugal–Bulgaria) and ran lines at both tournaments. His son **Edgardo Codesal**
  (Mexico) appears in 1986 as a linesman (Bulgaria–Italy opener and the Spain–Belgium
  quarter-final, per RSSSF; an inferred identification). Edgardo's Wikipedia article
  confirms the father–son link — and that he went on to referee the 1990 final, where he
  gave the late penalty against Argentina (that match is in the 1990–2010 batch).

## Known gaps

- 3 linesman slots in 1986 (see coverage table) — the only undocumented crew slots.
- 141 linesman slots carry surname + country only (host/club officials absent from all
  published panel lists); they are real, RSSSF-documented officials, not omissions.
- Linesman order (assistant1/assistant2) follows RSSSF's listing order; no source
  distinguishes a "senior" linesman in this era.
- The 1934 and 1938 Wikipedia articles publish no officials roster, so those tournaments'
  `tournament_officials` rows rest on fjelstul + match records alone.
- home_team/away_team follow fjelstul's fixture order throughout.
