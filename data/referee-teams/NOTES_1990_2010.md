# Officiating-crew data, FIFA Men's World Cups 1990–2010

Compiled 2026-07-17. Companion files (this batch covers 1990, 1994, 1998, 2002, 2006, 2010;
a separate batch covers 2014–2026):

- `tournament_officials_1990_2010.csv` — 399 rows: every appointed official per tournament
  (referees, assistants/linesmen, 2006 support group, plus selected-but-withdrawn officials, flagged in `role_pool`).
- `match_officials_1990_2010.csv` — 360 rows: one row per match (52+52+64+64+64+64),
  with referee, both assistants, and fourth official where documented.

Both CSVs are UTF-8 with BOM (open cleanly in Excel). `source_url` lists every source that
contributed to the row, separated by `; `.

pages, e.g. `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_C`,
`..._knockout_stage`, `..._final` for each tournament, plus two standalone match articles
(`United_States_v_Iran_(1998_FIFA_World_Cup)`, `Battle_of_Nuremberg_(2006_FIFA_World_Cup)`).

## How the officiating team was structured in each era

- **1990** — 41 match officials appointed as a single pool: every official was a qualified referee,
  and the two linesmen for each match were drawn from the same pool (12 of the 41 were used only as
  linesmen — flagged in the roster). No fourth official at the finals; there was no formal
  fourth-official role yet (IFAB created it in 1991).
- **1994** — first World Cup with **dedicated linesmen**: 24 referees + 22 specialist linesmen
  (the 24+22 split is stated in FIFA's own 1998 press release). Also the first World Cup with
  **fourth officials**, drawn from the referee pool (documented for all 36 group matches and the
  final; sources do not record fourth officials for the other 15 knockout matches).
- **1996** — IFAB renamed "linesman" to **"assistant referee"** (between the 1994 and 1998 cups).
- **1998** — 34 referees + 33 assistant referees, appointed as separate lists; crews were mixed
  nationality match-by-match (no fixed trios). Fourth officials from the referee pool.
  First finals with the "Signal Bip" flag-beep system.
- **2002** — 36 referees + 36 assistant referees, same mixed-crew model; assistants were typically
  paired so that referee, AR1 and AR2 came from three different associations.
- **2006** — first World Cup with **fixed national trios**: 21 trios at the tournament (23 selected),
  each referee with two dedicated assistants from his own country (or same confederation where a
  country couldn't supply both). Also introduced: a 15-man **support and development group**
  (5 referees + 10 assistants), the **fifth official** (reserve AR), and referee–assistant
  radio communication. Fourth officials were drawn from the other trios' referees.
- **2010** — same trio model: 30 trios announced (Feb 2010), reduced to 29 when the Amarilla and
  Benouza trios were removed (27 May 2010, assistants failed fitness tests) and the Vázquez trio
  was called up. Fourth official = a referee from another trio; fifth official = one of that
  referee's assistants. Pool cut to 19 trios from the quarter-finals.

## The 1994 linesman pool (reconstruction)

No official list of the 22 appointed 1994 linesmen survives online. The pool in
`tournament_officials_1990_2010.csv` was reconstructed from the per-match records
(Wikipedia group pages give full names; RSSSF knockout records give surnames, which were matched
back to the same people). The reconstruction yields **exactly 22 distinct linesmen**, matching
FIFA's stated count — a strong sign it is complete. Confederations for these rows use 1994-era
membership (Australia = OFC). Valentin Ivanov (Russia), later the 2006 referee, appears here as a
1994 linesman.

## Panel changes and withdrawn officials (flagged in `role_pool`)

- 1998: FIFA's February 1998 list differs from the final panel — Charles Masembe (Uganda) was
  replaced by Ian McLeod (South Africa) and Anders Frisk (Sweden) by Ryszard Wójcik (Poland).
  The roster carries the final panel; assistants are as per the February list (the only surviving list).
- 2006: Massimo De Santis (Italy — withdrawn by the FIGC over the Serie A scandal, not replaced;
  his selected assistants Alessandro Griselli and Marco Ivaldi did not serve) and Peter Prendergast
  (Jamaica — knee injury; assistants Anthony Garwood and Joseph Taylor did not serve). At the
  tournament Roberto Rosetti's trio was Copelli + Stagnoli, exactly as in FIFA's May 2006 release.
  Earlier in selection, Carlos Batres (injury; replaced by Marco Rodríguez) and the trios of
  Kyros Vassaras and Manuel Mejuto González (assistants failed tests; replaced by Rosetti and
  Medina Cantalejo) had already dropped out — those five never appear in the release list used here.
- 2010: Carlos Amarilla (Paraguay) and Mohamed Benouza (Algeria) rows are flagged as removed;
  their assistants are not named in any surviving source.

## Cross-check results and documented corrections

RSSSF vs Wikipedia agreement: referee identity agrees for all 360 matches (35 RSSSF spelling
variants, e.g. "Junini" for Jouini, "Belgola" for Belqola). For 1990/94/98 assistants the two
sources agree on personnel in 167 of 168 matches (RSSSF has many misspellings but the same people).

Corrections applied to the CSV (each verified against an official/independent source):

1. **Argentina v Brazil, 24 Jun 1990** — RSSSF lists linesmen Pairetto + Jouini; Wikipedia and
   planetworldcup.com both say **Pairetto + Alexey Spirin**. Spirin used. (RSSSF error.)
2. **Saudi Arabia v Denmark, 12 Jun 1998** — Wikipedia names an assistant "Jorge Diaz Garcia";
   the official france98.com report and FIFA's list say **DIAZ GALVEZ, Jorge Alberto** (Chile);
   RSSSF agrees. "Jorge Alberto Diaz Galvez" used for all his matches. (Wikipedia error.)
3. **"Reynaldo Salinas" (Honduras, 1998)** — official france98.com reports say
   **SALINAS ROSTRAN, Elias** (one report uses the initial "R.", so his full name may be
   Elias Reynaldo Salinas Rostran). "Elias Salinas Rostran" used. (Wikipedia first-name variant.)
4. **"Nimal Wickramatunge" (Sri Lanka, 1998)** — official reports and FIFA's list say
   **WICKERAMATUNGE SAMARATHNA, Muhamad**. Official form used. (Wikipedia first-name variant.)
5. **"Davoud Fanaei" (Iran, 1994)** — wrong wikilink on Wikipedia group pages: Davoud Fanaei is a
   goalkeeper born 1975. The 1994 linesman is **Mohammad Fanaei** (AR in the 1994 final).
6. Two RSSSF date typos corrected from Wikipedia: 1990 third-place match (7 July, not 7 June);
   Ecuador v Costa Rica 2006 (15 June, not 14 June).
7. Minor spelling variants across Wikipedia pages were harmonised to one form per person per
   tournament (55 substitutions, e.g. Al-Zeid/Al-Zaid, "John Toro Lendon" → John Toro Rendón,
   Stagnelli → Stagnoli, "Jeom Young Hyun" → Jeon Young-Hyun, "Esse" → Esfandiar Baharmast).

Name alias to be aware of when joining the two CSVs: the Brazilian 1998 assistant appears as
**"Arnaldo Pinto"** in `match_officials` (as on the match pages) and as
**"Arnaldo de Menezes Pinto Filho"** in `tournament_officials` (his full name per FIFA's list).

## Known gaps

- 1994 knockout-stage fourth officials (15 matches): not documented in any accessible source.
- 1990: no fourth officials existed; RSSSF's linesman order was kept as assistant1/assistant2 —
  neither source distinguishes "senior" assistant for this era.
- 1994 knockout assistants: RSSSF records only surnames for these 16 matches; full names were
  recovered by matching those surnames (with country) against the group-stage pool. All resolved —
  every crew slot in the match CSV now carries a full name.
- 2010 removed-trio assistants (Amarilla's and Benouza's): names not published.
- 2006/2010 fifth officials: captured but not in the CSV schema (see above).
- home_team/away_team follow the official fixture order (Wikipedia); RSSSF sometimes lists the
  winner first (e.g. the 2010 final), so RSSSF ordering was not used.

## Notable, for the Argentina angle

- Every Argentina match 1990–2010 has its full referee + assistants crew in the CSV.
- 1990 final (West Germany v Argentina): Edgardo Codesal (Mexico) with Pérez Hoyos (Colombia) and
  Listkiewicz (Poland). Listkiewicz ran the line in three Argentina matches in 1990 (opening match,
  semi-final, final) — the linesman for both Argentina heartbreak penalties of Italia 90.
- Michel Vautrot (France) refereed both Argentina's opening loss to Cameroon and the semi-final vs Italy in 1990.
- 2006 and 2010: Argentina's round-of-16 win over Mexico was refereed by the same man's trio-era
  counterparts twice — Massimo Busacca (2006) and Roberto Rosetti (2010); Ravshan Irmatov
  (Uzbekistan) took Argentina twice in 2010 (Greece group match and the 0–4 QF vs Germany), with
  Jerome Damon (South Africa) as fourth official in three of Argentina's five 2010 matches.
- Frank De Bleeckere (Belgium) with assistants Hermans + Vromans officiated Argentina's opener in
  both 2006 (v Ivory Coast) and 2010 (v South Korea — Argentina's second group game).
- Horacio Elizondo (Argentina) refereed the 2006 opening match and the 2006 final (Zidane red card);
  Argentine officials in the data: Elizondo trio (Darío García/Rodolfo Otero, 2006), Baldassi trio
  (Hernán Maidana/Ricardo Casas, 2010), Lamolina (1994 referee, and fourth official of the 1994 final),
  Loustau (1990 referee), Castrilli + assistant Claudio Rossi (1998), Ernesto Taibi (1994 linesman).
